COGIC's 118th Holy Convocation Closes in St. Louis After 8 Days of Prayer, Preaching and 'Answering the Call'

It began with an all-night prayer service and ended Tuesday, with Presiding Bishop J. Drew Sheard telling the saints to know the difference between what is trending and what is truth. Were you in St. Louis this week?

ST. LOUIS, Missouri · November 11, 2026 at 4:45 AM Pacific · By the Gospel Desk, Gospel News Access

It started with saints on their knees all night. It ended with them heading home ready to go.

The Church of God in Christ closed its 118th Holy Convocation in St. Louis on Tuesday, November 10, after eight days of business sessions, worship services and music at America's Center Convention Complex, according to COGIC and KSDK. This year's theme was "Answering the Call."

It began in prayer

Day One opened with a pre-convention meeting and then an all-night prayer service that COGIC says set the tone for the week. Presiding Bishop J. Drew Sheard, Mother Frances Kelley and Bishop Larry Perkins helped lead it, according to the church's official recap.

The mornings belonged to the Word. Supt. Courtney Sanders Jr. brought "Detour To My Destiny" at Morning Manna on Day Three, and Bishop Sheard himself taught "What We Believe" on Day Four, COGIC reported.

A word for this moment

The St. Louis American reported that Bishop Sheard warned the saints about social media and urged them to "discern between what is trending and what is truth." It is a word for all of us, GNA family, not just those in the convention hall.

Faith with its sleeves rolled up

Before the first service, COGIC took the gospel to the streets. The St. Louis American reported a community giveaway with the St. Louis Urban League, World Vision and Catholic Charities, bringing truckloads of clothes, toys and other items to St. Louis families. The paper also reported a push this year on Black men's health.

Why St. Louis

COGIC's General Assembly voted to hold the Convocation in St. Louis from 2026 through 2028 after the church could not reach satisfactory agreements with Memphis leaders and the local hospitality industry, according to COGIC and the Tri-State Defender. Explore St. Louis estimates about $31 million in local spending from this year's gathering.

"St. Louis has welcomed the Church of God in Christ with open arms, and we are grateful to return to this great city," Bishop Sheard said in the church's announcement.

The roots

COGIC was built under Bishop Charles Harrison Mason, and its mother church, Mason Temple in Memphis, is where Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. preached "I've Been to the Mountaintop" on April 3, 1968, the night before he was killed. That is the heritage that filled a convention center this week.

"Here am I; send me" (Isaiah 6:8).

The Convocation returns to St. Louis next November. Saints, what word from this year is still ringing in your spirit?
